FRUIT CHARACTERS. A short animated episode starring the product's own approved fruit character (its bible is below the facts): witty, warm, brand-owned, never mocking the fruit or the customer. It is animated from the first line to the last: every line plays over a character_scene shot, never a photo or the real fruit. Structure: the character's hook (the character speaks in its own personality; kind narrator, voiced by its house voice) → one or two short character beats → the last line, in character, names the product → the offer card follows. Keep to the character's do's and don'ts. Facts only from the approved list; the character never claims to be a real fruit, a customer or a farmer.

The character TALKS, it doesn't announce: whole, natural sentences in its own voice (from its bible), the way it would chat with someone who just walked into its kitchen, with contractions and real reactions ("Oh, hi! You're early. I wasn't even ready yet."). Never clipped labels or commands strung together ("Juice dripping. Score it. Taste it."); feedback 2026-11-04: "do natural speech on cartoon as well". Under 8 seconds a line. No ad words, no list of adjectives, and never a slogan.

JOKES LAND. One clear joke per video, said with confidence: a big, obvious comparison or pun about this fruit's own look, taste or texture that a viewer gets in one hearing (cherimoya: "Smell that? That's me. Custard in a green jacket."). Never apologise for a joke or explain it ("Ha, sorry", "get it?"), never a pun that only works written down (scents/cents), and never a joke about something the shot doesn't show.
THE CHARACTER IS THE FRUIT, so it never talks about being bitten, chewed or eaten, and never about the viewer's face, bite or reaction it can't see (feedback 2026-11-09: "Oh, look at your face! You weren't ready for that bite" was bad). Open on something the character is doing or showing in its scene: it shows off its colour, its juice, its box. Never say "cartoon", "animated" or "character" out loud.
