# Meta Creative Playbook

Sample playbook

## How to use this playbook

This is the house standard for every Meta ad the brand ships, statics and video. Ad Studio turns it into its quality rubric, its prompts and its pre-launch checks. People use it to brief, review and judge creative.

**The 10 rules that matter most**

1. **Test concepts, not tweaks.** A new angle or format is a new ad; a new colour or crop is not.
2. **Win the first second.** Product or hook on screen immediately, readable with the sound off.
3. **Show the fruit, open.** The cut, the colour, the texture. Appetite appeal beats lifestyle polish.
4. **One message per ad.** One focal point, one claim, one CTA.
5. **Build for 9:16 and 4:5 natively,** with text inside the safe zones.
6. **High contrast, few words.** Text on a static should read at thumbnail size in under 2 seconds.
7. **Proof beats adjectives.** Real reviews, ripeness promises and guarantees, never vague superlatives.
8. **Every claim comes from the approved facts library.** Prices only from Shopify.
9. **Answer the objection.** Ripeness, damage and shipping worries are the reasons people don't buy.
10. **Read results by pattern.** Judge angles, hooks and formats, not single ads, and retire what fatigues.

## How Meta delivery works now, and what it means for us

Meta's AI picks who sees each ad based on what the creative shows, so the creative is now the main targeting lever. Our job is to give it many clearly different ads and clean signals.

| What Meta does (2026) | What it means for production |
|---|---|
| Andromeda retrieval groups near-identical ads into one "entity", so 10 colour or crop tweaks act like about 1 ad | Make variants differ in angle, format or scene, not in small details |
| Rewards 20–30 genuinely different creatives per ad set; one diverse ad set beat five similar ones on conversions and cost in a cited test | Fewer, broader ad sets, each fed with diverse concepts |
| Stories and Reels share one 9:16 safe zone since March 2026; Meta steers feed to 4:5 and 9:16 | Export 9:16 and 4:5 natively; 1:1 only as a fallback |
| Advantage+ creative "enhancements" are on by default: crops, added music, moved or animated text | Turn off enhancements that touch logo, product photos and approved copy |
| AI-made content gets an "AI info" label (C2PA detection); undisclosed AI content is a rejection reason; photoreal AI people get the most visible label | Flag every AI asset; AI presenters are brand narrators, never fake customers |
| Ads peak in week one, then plateau | Keep a steady flow of new concepts; refresh statics every 2–3 weeks |

The AI optimises on the purchase data we send, so Conversions API and match quality matter as much as creative.

## Concepts vs variations

Spend testing money on concepts; let Meta choose between variations. About 5% of ads become winners, so the number of genuinely different concepts matters more than polishing one.

**The three layers of an ad**

| Layer | What it is | Example | Changing it = |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Angle** | Why someone buys | "Fruit you've never tasted", "the gift that gets talked about", "tree-ripened, not warehouse-ripened" | A new concept |
| **Format** | How it's packaged | Cut-open static, review card, Fruit Story video, talking head, us vs supermarket | A new concept |
| **Hook** | The first line or first second | "Most mangoes never ripen", a knife going through a dragon fruit | A variation, unless the angle changes too |

**Working rules**

- A tired account needs a new angle, not new hooks.
- Test 6–10 distinct concepts per batch. Put each concept's 3–5 best variations in one ad or ad set and let Meta pick.
- Spend 10–20% of budget on testing once winners exist; more while searching.
- Judge a concept after it has spent 1–2× target CPA, read weekly, after 7 days: graduate, iterate or retire.
- Change one variable at a time when iterating a proven winner: hook, then visual, then offer.

## Static ads

A great static shows the fruit cut open, says one thing in a few large words, and proves it. Statics are cheap to make and last longer than video, so they carry most of our testing volume.

**Formats that work, and when to use them**

| Format | Use it when | Our version |
|---|---|---|
| **Appetite close-up** | Always the default; close-ups last longest of all food formats (about 12 days) | Macro cross-section of mango or dragon fruit + one taste line |
| **Review / testimonial card** | Trust is the barrier; real, specific praise exists | Verified Google review quote over the fruit it praises |
| **Us vs them** | Buyers already have an alternative | Pale supermarket mango vs tree-ripened mango, cut side by side |
| **Offer stack** | Launches, bundles, gifting peaks; works best on warm audiences | Variety box contents + price per fruit + free shipping + guarantee |
| **Problem–solution** | Cold traffic | "Bland, unripe fruit?" → the box → ripening guarantee |
| **Advertorial / explainer card** | The product needs a little education | "5 fruits you've never tried" with one line each |
| **Gift reveal / flat lay** | Gifting season, gift buyers | Open box from above, labelled fruits, gift message |
| **Carousel** | Several products or a story in steps; multi-image formats hold up well | One card per fruit in the box, or harvest → pack → doorstep |

**Design rules**

- **Product first.** The fruit fills 40–60% of the frame, cut open where it helps. Packaging on white is the weakest food format; use it only for recognition.
- **One focal point,** one message, one CTA.
- **Few words.** Headline of 3–7 words; total on-image text under about 20 words.
- **Contrast.** Text-to-background contrast of at least 4.5:1 (WCAG AA); use a solid or shaded panel behind text over busy fruit. The subject must stand apart from its background at thumbnail size.
- **Warm, natural light,** real surfaces, saturated but true fruit colour. No bleached studio white.
- **Text never covers the fruit.** Logo small, in a corner.
- **Readable at phone size.** Check it at 25% zoom.

**Sizes and safe zones**

| Placement | Size | Keep text and logo clear of |
|---|---|---|
| Feed (default) | 4:5, 1080×1350 (or 1440×1800) | Outer 5% on each side |
| Stories / Reels | 9:16, 1080×1920 | Top 14%, bottom 35%, sides 6% (unified safe zone since March 2026) |
| Fallback | 1:1, 1080×1080 | Outer 5% |

Design each size natively. Never let Meta crop a 1:1 into 9:16.

## Video ads

A great video shows fruit in the first second, changes something on screen about every 2 seconds, makes sense with the sound off, and pays off its hook before asking for the order.

**Rules**

- **First 1–3 seconds decide everything.** Open on the most visual moment (knife through fruit, juice, the reveal) or the hook line on screen. Under 2 seconds for Stories.
- **Sound off first.** Most Facebook video is watched muted. Burn in captions for every spoken word, at least 24 pt, high contrast, centred or lower third, inside the safe zone.
- **Sound on second.** Reels viewers often listen: use real sounds (cutting, crunch, orchard ambience) and a voice that adds information, not repetition.
- **Keep it moving.** A visual, text or audio change about every 2 seconds; no single shot past about 3 seconds between seconds 5 and 15.
- **Deliver by second 5.** The body proves the hook early: taste, texture, the guarantee, a review.
- **CTA in the last 3–5 seconds** for Feed; earlier for Stories, before people tap away.
- **Natural beats polished.** Creator-style and real settings outperform studio gloss on Reels.
- **Build modular.** Separate hooks, bodies and end cards so one body can carry 3–5 hooks.

**Length by placement**

| Placement | Length | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Reels | 6–30 s (sweet spot 9–20 s) | 9:16, 1080×1920 |
| Stories | 6–15 s | 9:16, 1080×1920 |
| Feed, prospecting | 15–30 s | 4:5 or 9:16 |
| Retargeting | 15–60 s | 4:5 or 9:16 |

Export MP4 (H.264, AAC, 30 fps, at least 8 Mbps at 1080p).

**Brand video structures**

| Style | Length | Shot order |
|---|---|---|
| **Fruit Story (flagship, merges the old "Storytelling")** | 20–45 s | Hook (0–3 s) → orchard → fruit on the tree → 3 fast cuts and textures → why it's different → offer card. Two versions: **presenter-led** (an AI narrator on camera, with cutaways) or **narrated** (voice-over only). These were one idea, so they're one style with a switch |
| **Talking head / explainer** | 15–30 s | Presenter states the surprising fact → explains one idea (how to eat it, why it tastes different) → proof → CTA |
| **Sensory / ASMR** | 8–15 s | Macro cut with real sound → texture → juice → end card; no voice |
| **Animated still** | 5–8 s | One approved image with gentle motion + headline; ideal for Stories |
| **Us vs supermarket** | 10–20 s | Split screen: both fruits cut → colour and texture compared → "picked when ripe" → CTA |
| **POV unboxing / gift reveal** | 10–20 s | Doorstep → lid off → fruit reveal → cut → shared at the table → CTA |

New styles to test are in the next section, "Styles to test next".

Unboxing and creator-style videos get the highest click-through in food but tire fastest, around 9 days. Refresh their first 3 seconds first.

## Styles to test next

Our first list was all product-realistic footage. Three whole families were missing: **animation**, **native lo-fi formats** and **education**. Each looks nothing like the rest of the feed, which is what Meta's diversity rules reward. Test them as concepts, one or two at a time.

**Why now:** in a large analysis of Meta spend, text-only ads, product images with text overlays and GIFs produced more winners than high-production ads. Letters (1.87×), unconventional text placement (1.44×) and post-its (1.38×) earned more than their share of spend, and "newness" and sale-announcement hooks had the highest hit rates (about 11%).

| Priority | Style | Static / video | Why test it | How Ad Studio makes it | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fruit characters (cartoon / 3D)** | Video 10–20 s | AI fruit "dramas" pulled 10M+ views per episode on TikTok in March 2026; a brand-owned character (e.g. a mango personality) is a strong pattern interrupt | Character sheet in Nano Banana Pro → Kling/Seedance shots → voice → timeline; **always end on the real fruit, cut open** | Animation lifts awareness but live action wins purchase intent for physical products (about 35% in one comparison), so use it as a hook and top-of-funnel style; the trend is criticised as "AI slop", so keep it witty, brand-owned and labelled |
| 2 | **Claymation / stop-motion** | Video 10–20 s | Nothing else in the feed looks like it; food stop-motion is a proven classic (PES "Fresh Guacamole") | Clay-style stills → image-to-video; e.g. a clay mango sliced open to reveal the real fruit inside | Practitioners claim 2–3× CTR, unverified; generate at about $3–6 an ad, so cheap to test |
| 3 | **Ripening time-lapse** | Video 6–15 s | Answers our biggest objection (ripeness) visually: firm on day 1, perfect on day 3 | Image-to-video from approved stills of each ripeness stage, with day labels | Must match the real ripening guide and approved facts |
| 4 | **Native lo-fi statics** | Static | Look like a friend's post, not an ad; low effort, high volume | Notes-app list, text-message thread, post-it, handwritten letter, screenshot of real comments, review mashup, all rendered as Compose layouts | **Only real reviews and comments;** no invented conversations presented as genuine |
| 5 | **Harvest drop / announcement + offer-first banner** | Static + animated | Newness and announcement hooks had the highest hit rates; offer-first banners scale well | Harvest calendar + Shopify stock → "Lychee season is here" banner and countdown | Urgency must be real (actual season dates and stock) |
| 6 | **How to eat it / FAQ** | Video 15–30 s + carousel | Exotic fruit has a real learning barrier ("how do I open a mangosteen?") | Presenter or hands-only how-to; carousel of steps | Facts from the library only |
| 7 | **Comment reply** | Video 10–20 s | Native format; answers objections like "is it worth $54?" in public | Presenter replies to a real comment shown as a sticker | Only real comments, with the commenter's name hidden |
| 8 | **Listicle / quiz** | Carousel or video | "5 fruits you've never tried" or "which exotic fruit are you?" drives discovery | Product Hub data → one card per fruit | Keep each card to one line |
| 9 | **Fast-cut montage** | Video 6–10 s | Shows the whole range in seconds; good for variety boxes | Shot library clips cut to music | Needs licensed music |
| 10 | **Meme / trend formats** | Static or video | Cheap reach when a format is hot | Template from the weekly research brief | Ages fast; use only current formats |

**Need real people or footage, so not AI-made:** customer reaction videos, street interviews, testimonials on camera, and the farmer or founder story. These work well in food, but faking them breaks Meta's rules and the FTC's fake-review rule. Add them when the brand can film real footage.

## Hooks

The hook is the first line of a static or the first 1–3 seconds of a video, and it decides whether anything else is seen. Aim for a hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) above 30%; under 15% means rebuild the opening.

**Hook types, with examples**

| Type | Why it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual / sensory** | Texture and motion stop the thumb before words are read | Knife through a pink mango, juice running; dragon fruit split open |
| **Curiosity** | An open question the viewer wants closed | "This fruit tastes like custard. Most people have never heard of it." |
| **Contrarian / negative** | Mistakes and warnings grab more attention than benefits | "Stop buying mangoes at the supermarket." |
| **Problem** | Names a frustration the viewer already feels | "Tired of fruit that never ripens?" |
| **Social proof / FOMO** | Others chose it, and it may run out | "2,400+ Google reviews. Lychee season ends in 3 weeks." |
| **Discovery / list** | Promises something new, fast | "5 fruits you've never tried (number 3 tastes like candy)" |
| **Gift** | Ties the product to a moment | "The gift my dad still talks about." |
| **Offer** | Direct value; best on warm audiences | "Free mango with your first box." |

**Rules**

- **Show and say at once.** The best hooks pair a striking fruit visual with on-screen words.
- **Pay it off.** A strong hook with weak hold means the body didn't deliver; that's clickbait.
- **Be specific.** Numbers, fruit names and real moments beat adjectives.
- **Break the pattern.** Familiar polished openings get scrolled past.
- **One body, many hooks.** Test 3–5 (up to 10) hooks on one winning body.
- **Refresh hooks first** when a winner fades; ads that once lasted about 3 weeks now last 5–7 days on hooks alone.
- **Never imply a personal condition** ("Are you struggling with…"). Meta rejects personal-attribute copy.

## Copy, CTA and compliance

Copy should add what the image can't say: proof, the promise, and the reason to act now. Keep it short and true.

**Field rules**

| Field | Length | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Primary text | 50–150 characters for cold audiences; 250–400 for warm | Hook in the first 125 characters (the rest hides behind "See more") |
| Headline | Aim for 27 characters, max 40 | Adds something new: removes an objection or compresses proof; never repeats the primary text |
| Description | Up to 27–30 characters | Optional; a guarantee or shipping line |
| Variations | Up to 5 per field | Each a different angle, not a rewording |

**Copy frameworks**

- **Problem → agitate → solution:** cold audiences.
- **Benefit → proof → CTA:** warm audiences.
- **Review-led:** a real customer quote, then the product, then the CTA.
- **Curiosity:** an unfinished thought with a real payoff.
- **Urgency:** only with a real deadline or real stock limit (harvest season counts).

**CTA rules**

- **One CTA per ad,** matched to intent: *Shop now* or *Order now* for purchase; *Get offer* only when there is an actual offer; *Learn more* for explainer content.
- **The on-image CTA, the button and the landing page say the same thing.**
- **Give a reason to act now** that is true: season ending, gift deadline, free gift.
- **One or two emoji at most.**

**Compliance (non-negotiable)**

- **Facts only from the approved facts library; prices only from Shopify.**
- **Health and nutrition:** describe the fruit and its nutrients with a source; never claim to cure or treat, and qualify any result ("can help", not "will").
- **No personal-attribute copy** ("Are you diabetic?"). Speak to what people want, not what they are.
- **Reviews and testimonials must be real.** No invented, AI-written or paid-for-sentiment reviews; the FTC can seek up to $52,000 per violation. Attribute real quotes and keep their meaning.
- **AI disclosure:** AI people and AI-made scenes are flagged on upload. Presenters are brand narrators, never fake customers or a real farmer.
- **No false scarcity, bait pricing or "free" with hidden charges.** Meta also checks that the landing page matches the ad.

## What each variation tests

Change the variable that matches the question you're asking. Big variables (imagery type, format, angle) find new winners; small ones (headline, colour, CTA) only polish a proven ad.

| Change | What it tests | When to use it | Counts as new concept for Meta? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Angle** | Why people buy (taste, discovery, gift, freshness, value) | Account is tired; looking for new winners | Yes |
| **Format** | Static vs video vs carousel; talking head vs Fruit Story vs ASMR | Same angle, new audience pocket | Yes |
| **Imagery type** | Cut-open vs whole vs box vs lifestyle vs hand-held | First variable to test inside a concept | Usually yes |
| **Hook** | The opening line or first 3 seconds | Hook rate low, or a winner is fading | No: a variation |
| **Product** | Mango vs variety box vs gift basket | A concept won on one product | Yes, per product |
| **Offer** | Free gift, free shipping, membership, bundle | Concept gets attention but low conversion | No, unless the angle changes |
| **Proof element** | Review quote, rating, guarantee, ripening promise | Clicks without purchases | No |
| **Headline / copy** | Wording of the same idea | Polishing a proven winner | No |
| **Length** | 6 / 15 / 30 s cuts | Hold rate drops mid-video | No |
| **Size** | 9:16, 4:5, 1:1 | Always: ship all native sizes | No: same ad |
| **Presenter / voice** | Who delivers the message | Talking-head winner fading | Partly |
| **Colour, crop, CTA button** | Minor visual tweaks | Last; rarely worth a test | No |

**Order of testing for a new product:** offer (if it's in question) → angle → imagery type and format → hook → proof → copy. A weak offer can't be rescued by good creative, so settle it first.

## What works in food and specialty fruit

Food wins on appetite, proof and occasion. Shoppers can't taste or smell online, so the ad must make them imagine it and trust it.

**Patterns to use**

- **Appetite-first close-ups and ASMR:** cross-sections, juice, slow motion, real sound, often no dialogue.
- **Unboxing and creator-style video:** the highest click-through in food (about 1.55% modelled), but it tires in about 9 days.
- **Comparison:** Misfits Market sets its price beside a rival's; for us, colour and ripeness beside a supermarket fruit.
- **Contrarian proof:** Burger King's "Moldy Whopper" proved no preservatives by letting it rot. Our lighter version: "we don't pick it green".
- **Occasion over ingredients:** breakfast, gifting, a family treat, nostalgia ("the mango from my childhood").
- **Social proof:** reviews and ratings can lift click-through by 20–40%.
- **Seasonal drops:** harvest windows give real urgency ("lychee season, 3 weeks only").
- **Volume:** the median food brand runs about 150 active Meta ads; plan 10–15 new ads a month at minimum.

**Category benchmarks (modelled estimates, use as rough guides)**

| Segment | CTR | CPA | ROAS | Creative life |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food category baseline | 1.10% | $22 | 4.5× | 11 days |
| Specialty & gifting | 0.85% | $34 | 3.5× | 14 days |
| Appetite close-up format | 1.30% | $20 | 4.6× | 12 days |
| Packaging on white | 0.70% | $31 | 3.6× | 15 days |

CPMs peak in November and December; plan gifting creative 6–8 weeks ahead.

**What the brand should own:** rare-fruit discovery ("you've never tasted this"), tree-ripened taste, and the gift that gets talked about. Answer the category's known objections every time: ripeness, damage in transit and delays. Competitors like Miami Fruit get the same complaints, so proof here is a real advantage.

## Metrics and diagnosis

Read results by pattern (angle, hook, format, product), after at least 7 days and about 5,000 impressions per ad. Day 1–2 numbers are noise.

**The metrics**

| Metric | Formula | Cold-traffic benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Hook rate | 3-second views ÷ impressions | Below 15% rebuild; 25–35% solid; above 35% strong (Reels median about 30%) |
| Hold rate | 15-second views ÷ 3-second views | Below 10% weak; 15–25% healthy; 30%+ best in class |
| ThruPlay rate | ThruPlays ÷ plays | 15–30 s videos: about 20% median; compare sub-15 s only with each other |
| CTR (link) | Link clicks ÷ impressions | Food baseline about 1.1%; specialty and gifting about 0.85% |
| CPA / ROAS | Spend ÷ purchases; revenue ÷ spend | Judge against our own targets, new customers separately |

**Diagnosis**

| Pattern | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low hook, low hold | Opening doesn't stop anyone | Rewrite the first 1–3 s; don't touch the body yet |
| High hook, low hold | Body doesn't pay off the hook | Faster pacing, proof by second 5, cut dead air |
| Good hold, low CTR | No reason to act | Stronger CTA, offer or urgency |
| Good CTR, low conversion | Promise and page don't match, or the offer is weak | Align ad and landing page; test the offer |
| All strong, weak ROAS | Engagement isn't purchase intent | Check conversion tracking and audience; test the offer |

**Fatigue: refresh when two of these trip**

- Frequency above 3.5 in 7 days (Feed) or 3.0 (Reels and Stories).
- Engagement down more than 25% from week one.
- Cost per result up more than 30% from the first two weeks.

Refresh hooks first, then imagery, then retire the concept.

## Pre-launch checklists

Every ad passes its checklist before export. Ad Studio checks the items marked (auto); a person checks the rest.

**Static**

- Fruit is accurate: shape, flesh colour, seeds or stone match the reference photos (auto)
- Fruit fills 40–60% of the frame; one focal point
- Headline 3–7 words; total on-image text under about 20 words
- Text contrast at least 4.5:1, readable at 25% zoom (auto)
- Text doesn't cover the fruit; logo small, in a corner (auto)
- Text inside the safe zone for every size: 9:16, 4:5, 1:1 (auto)
- Every claim is an approved fact; price matches Shopify (auto)
- One CTA, matching the button and the landing page
- Angle, hook and format tagged; asset ID set as the Meta ad name (auto)
- AI-generated flag set (auto)

**Video**

- Fruit or hook on screen in the first second
- Makes sense with sound off; captions burned in, 24 pt or larger, inside the safe zone (auto)
- Something changes about every 2 seconds; no dead air between 5 and 15 s
- Hook paid off by second 5
- Fruit count and shape stay consistent from first to last frame (auto)
- Length fits the placement (Reels 9–20 s sweet spot; Stories 6–15 s)
- Presenter lines use approved facts only; AI person flagged (auto)
- Music licensed; CTA in the last 3–5 s
- Exported 9:16 and 4:5, MP4 H.264, 30 fps (auto)

**How this feeds Ad Studio:** each "auto" item maps to a criterion in `config/qa_rubric.yaml` or a pre-export check. The hook types, formats and video structures above become the tags and presets used in Images, Video and the idea bank. When this playbook changes, the rubric is updated to match, with the owner's approval.
